# Boreal life dossier

Scope: current winter boreal scene, not a complete northern population model.
Morphology remains in `docs/LIFE.md` and the sprite modules.

## European roe deer

The current proof is one adult male *Capreolus capreolus* in winter. Winter roe
deer may be solitary or form fluid groups, but no group is inferred for this
individual. Birch and *Rubus*-like browse, sheltered snow, edge cover, quiet,
and reachable dry ground define local use. Dawn/dusk activity affects
encounter, not occupancy.

Fear is contextual and hysteretic: orient or freeze can precede flight. The
separate aggression contract permits holding ground but does not imply combat.
Immediate cutting removes browse and cover and increases exposure; later light
and deciduous release may add browse before regrowth restores cover. Cone loss
belongs to the squirrel response, not the roe-deer food relation.

Non-claims: herd dynamics, density, carrying capacity, snow energetics, births,
deaths, or a general cervid model.

## Eurasian wild boar

The identity is *Sus scrofa*. The scene compiles one solitary adult or declared
matrilineal unit, never two unrelated generic wanderers. Rooting substrate,
deadwood cover, quiet, and a northern-edge bias constrain occupancy. Low-light
activity raises the encounter diagnostic; because runtime realization does not
yet sample that diagnostic, the current cohort must not be described as a
measured or visually enforced rarity.

Boar can avoid active cutting/noise yet later use disturbed rooting substrate.
Fear and aggression are separate because the same animal may flee, hold,
display, or defend according to context.

Non-claims: fixed population, universal group size, generic hostility, or a
uniform response to tree removal.

## Eurasian red squirrel

The identity is *Sciurus vulgaris*. Individuals are solitary with overlapping
ranges. Spruce cone mast, canopy continuity, deadwood, and familiar anchors
matter. Winter activity is diurnal and weighted toward late morning rather
than the dark edges of a 62° N day. The readable action signature is
forage/cache followed by freeze, travel, or hide, not insect-like continuous
random steering.

Canopy and cone loss can displace an individual even where the ground remains
walkable. Paw tracks are current bounded evidence; cone scales remain declared
action-sign vocabulary until action-specific evidence emission is implemented.
Neither is a population count.

## Resource relationships

Spruce cones support squirrel forage/cache. Birch/bramble browse and sheltered
snow support roe-deer winter use. Rooting substrate supports boar. Reeds and
deadwood provide cover across guilds. Stumps, slash, seed trees, compacted
routes, and regrowth are disturbance legacies, not decoration.

Executable claim classes and non-goals are in `src/life.js`. Encoded laws and
their citations are in `docs/CANON.md`.
