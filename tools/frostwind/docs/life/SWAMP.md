# Swamp life dossier

The swamp is a hydrological mosaic. Depth, hydroperiod, flow, hummock/hollow
state, shallow prey, bask structures, refuge, traffic, and flood history remain
shared measurable authorities for plants, animals, traversal, and settlement.

## American alligator

The exact taxon is *Alligator mississippiensis*. Alligator holes are persistent
dry-season refugia and prey-concentration sites. The existing 17 m value is
renamed a local refuge influence catchment; it is not a biological territory or
home range.

Individuals may aggregate locally without forming a stable social group. They
may bask, float, forage, travel, hold/display/defend, or submerge. Drawdown
concentrates use at existing refuge; changing water level never manufactures a
new hole influence. Nearby traffic/noise can trigger submergence only when
reachable water exists, a dry-ground animal travels to water before changing
medium state. Familiar refuge is not by itself a defended territory.

## Large ardeid wader guild

The raster does not support species-level identification. Shallow prey,
wadeable depth, quiet margins, reeds, and perches constrain use. Individuals
may aggregate at resources; the compiler does not invent a pair bond or colony.
Drawdown can increase prey concentration while removing some reachable margins,
so response is derived from the same hydrology rather than a species toggle.
The current `flee` response is rapid departure through the wading bank, not a
claim that a flight animation exists; aerial escape remains a medium concession.

## Large basking emydid guild

The raster likewise supports a functional guild, not a taxon. Basking
structures, solar/thermal opportunity, shallow food, and escape water define
sites. Several individuals may share a bask structure without being a social
cohort. Traffic can cause submergence; changing water level moves both bask and
feeding sites.

## Sub-sprite guilds and people

Aquatic prey is an executable resource field even when no prey body is drawn.
Its role must be visible through predator use, drawdown response, traps, traces,
or diagnostics.

Fisher and boatwright are separate authored residents in one mutual-repair work
group. They are not presumed kin or friends. Water-as-road, flood memory, and
mutual repair are fictional cultural choices. Flood, heat, shallow water and
raised circulation constrain them without selecting them.

Architecture and hydrology detail remains in `docs/SWAMP.md`. Encoded laws and
their citations are in `docs/CANON.md`.

## The skimmer and the lamp snail

Two fictional animals, one invented law each, both pose tables on
`src/skeleton.js`. `docs/CANON.md` carries both rows.

The skimmer is a water strider (*Gigantometra gigas*, 36 mm) at five times its
length, the smallest scale at which its body draws three pixels. Its law is the held film: still water does not break under its feet.
What the law leaves alone is the animal: no film on land or on a weed mat, so
the bank is a wall; the real 1.5 m/s burst; one stroke of the middle legs and
a glide. It is a dark cross a metre wide on open water, with a pale glint
at each planted foot. It rows away from a walker and never onto the bank.

The lamp snail is the giant African land snail's body (*Lissachatina fulica*)
with the light of *Quantula striata*, the one land snail known to glow. Its
law is kept foxfire: it eats the glowing fungus of rotting wood and keeps the
light, as glow and as growth. It lives at `deadwood`, moves 1 cm a second,
flashes green one frame in two, and withdraws when a walker is a metre off. A
withdrawn snail shows no light.

Flight, predation on people, toxicity, reproduction, the fungus species and
population density are not claimed.
