# The Canon

Register of encoded laws, unsupported claims, deliberately refused folklore,
and available science. It is not a schedule.

Every citation must be checkable. Values from reference measurements or
secondary ranges are marked **informal**; **unverified** entries are leads, not
facts. Each encoded row names its implementation and relevant limits.

## Recorded as folklore, deliberately not encoded

Five real citations that were considered and refused. They are here so nobody
spends the day again finding out why.

| Claim | Why not |
|---|---|
| **The Kruithof curve** — dim light is pleasing only if warm | Kruithof, *Philips Technical Review* VI(3):65–73, 1941. Replication has repeatedly failed (Boyce & Cuttle 1990; Davis & Ginthner 1990; Han & Boyce 2003; Viénot et al. 2009), and Fotios, *LEUKOS* 13(1), 2017, collapses the pleasing region to a flat line at about 300 lux: CCT has negligible effect |
| **Birkhoff's aesthetic measure**, M = O/C | *Aesthetic Measure*, 1933. Empirically unsupported (APA record 1938-01510-001), and its monotone penalty on complexity is contradicted by the fractal-preference result |
| **The rule of thirds** | No perceptual support; eye-tracking finds an expert-preference effect, which is a finding about training |
| **Hambidge's dynamic symmetry** | *The Elements of Dynamic Symmetry*, 1926. The Greek attribution is rejected by classical scholarship — *The Art Bulletin* 3(3), 1920 |
| **A target emptiness ratio for *ma*** | No quantified account of how much emptiness a composition wants exists. The measurement is real and printed; the target would be invented |

Also refused: **"cool light ⇒ warm shadow"** as a reciprocal universal. Only
the two-illuminant mechanism is real (Rayleigh, *Phil. Mag.* 41:107–120,
1871), a shadow takes the colour of whichever light actually reaches it, which
is warm bounce sometimes and cool sky usually. And the aphorism *"value does
the reading, hue does the mood"* is **not** attributable to Munsell, Ruskin or
Dow; the citable form of it is Livingstone & Hubel 1988, above.

## Laws of the medium

The second canon, and the one that wins when the two conflict. These are as real
as the physics and more often violated, because they have no textbook.

| Law | Statement | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Legibility floor** | Below roughly eight pixels the parts of a drawing stop existing, so proportion stops being derivable from life and becomes derivable from readability | `docs/LIFE.md`; outdoor makers and figure banks in `src/scale.js` |
| **Rational projection** | A projection ratio must be a small rational or edges land on fractional pixels. ½ is the only one small enough here | `PROJECTION.md`, `src/scale.js` |
| **Quantised value count** | `posterise` with `uSteps = 3` returns four levels, so authoring a fifth continuous shade is authoring a number the pipeline discards | `uSteps` in `src/render.js` / `src/shaders/sprite.js`; outdoor ramps in `src/sunlight.js` |
| **Integer scale only** | A fractional zoom resamples the grid, which is the thing this project is an argument against | `fitZoom` in `src/main.js` |
| **The palette is closed** | No content path accepts a colour. Style is structural, not a matter of discipline. Some ramps are computed rather than authored, but a sprite still stores a material id | `src/palette.js`, `src/sunlight.js` |
| **The frame is the height authority** | A 180-row frame is 11.77 m tall at `PLAYER_H = 26 px = 1.70 m`, so nothing may exceed roughly half of it. Trees are drawn at 0.16–0.20× life for this reason and buildings at 1.0×, and the world had two vertical scales without noticing | `docs/MEADOW.md`; `worldgen.js`; `test/biome.test.mjs` |
| **A silhouette a viewer cannot name is the wrong silhouette** | The desert's camel was a dromedary, *Camelus dromedarius*, which is the species that belongs at 24 N, and at a 43 px canvas its single hump read as a swaybacked horse. The shape a viewer recognises as a camel has two humps. The rig is now a Bactrian, *Camelus bactrianus*, so the anatomy is honest and the cost moves from anatomy to RANGE: the Bactrian is a Central Asian cold-desert animal and this is a Saharan latitude. The domestic Bactrian is the caravan animal of the Asian desert roads, which is what makes it defensible beside a culture built on caravans, and it is still a stretch. Naming it is the price of making it | `CAMEL_LIFE` in `src/body-camel.js` carries the species and the two crowns with the saddle between; `bodyPass` in `src/figure-camel.js` draws one outline over three arcs, because drawing each hump separately measured back as crown-notch-plateau-notch-crown. Asserted twice in `test/camel.test.mjs`, on the pose and on the drawn silhouette, and the second test exists because the pose was right while the pixels were wrong |
| **A finer ramp needs less dithering, not more** | Ordered dither across the largest surface in a frame is noise, not gradient. Winter's snow rungs are 32 luminance apart and its ground mostly sits ON a rung; the sward's are 23 apart and the same field crossed a boundary almost everywhere, giving 9.4% single-colour islands against winter's 1.9% | `SURFACE[].dither`, `src/surface.js` |
| **A ramp may not be longer than the posterise ladder** | `uSteps` is 3, so a posterised value takes one of four levels and `rampStep` rounds `t·(rungs−1)`. At seven rungs those four land on 0, 2, 4 and 6 and three of the seven colours can never appear. A ramp longer than the ladder is not a richer ramp, it is a ramp with holes in it — richness is several four-rung families | Sprint 3, the `sward` split into `canopy` and `grass`; the recovered summer palette reached the same conclusion from the same fact |
| **Two ramps of one family share rungs exactly, or clear each other** | `POST_FS` snaps to the nearest entry of the whole scene palette, so a ramp landing INSIDE another's step steals its pixels — and a rung-to-rung collision test cannot see it, because the colliding value is not a rung. Measured: with `canopy` and `grass` merely overlapping in range, 72.6% of an open meadow frame was one colour and that colour was a foliage rung painted over the ground | `loam`'s note in `src/sunlight.js` states the rule. Meadow `canopy`/`grass` clear each other as separate foliage/sward `makeRamp`s; swamp water windows still share exactly. Asserted in `test/biome.test.mjs` |
| **The aerial treatment is taste, and it belongs to the scene** | Airlight over the thirty metres a screen covers is six parts in ten thousand — the physical term at this scale is zero, so `aerialApply` is a glare and contrast treatment and must say so. Its four numbers were literals in `W_GROUND_FS`, and 150 is the snowfield's own luminance: over a meadow they pushed the ground clean off its own ramp | Sprint 3, `uAer` and `SCENES.aer`. Found by measuring, not by reading: the ground plane had not been painted from the sward ramp since the biome was built |
| **A dither seam is a pixel budget, not a width in t** | `rampDither` steepened `fract(f)` through a band 0.12 of a rung wide, and how many PIXELS that buys depends entirely on how fast the field crosses a rung. Tuned on the snowfield, which sweeps a rung every fifteen pixels or so, it read as the intended four or five. Measured on swamp water — a solution basin under a saturating optical curve — the same 0.12 bought **0.38 px** at the median boundary pixel and 0.15 px at the steep decile: a rung boundary with no pixel inside its own seam, which is a hard cut with the dither quantiser switched on above it. The width is now the local slope times `SEAM_PX`, read from the geom texture's own neighbours rather than from `fwidth`, since SwiftShader is a target and `dFdx` is not core in the GLSL ES 1.0 profile these compile as. 0.12 stays as the FLOOR, so a field slower than the budget keeps the seam it was tuned to and the snowfield does not move; `SEAM_MAX` is the ceiling, because a field that steps in one pixel is a real edge — a trail's border, a shadow's rim — and must stay one | `SEAM_PX`, `fieldSlope` in `src/shaders/world.js` |
| **A tone boundary inside one body of water is a seam, never a cut** | A picture may put an edge only where the world has one, and a pond bottom is continuous. Measured on the lakehouse basin, a bright line lay against a dark one for **971 px** along the shore with flat teal either side. The cause was upstream of the quantiser: the swamp ramps are a five-rung ladder derived from `swampRadiance`, and the bake indexed them with two straight-line fits that put one boundary at 0.006 m, the next at 0.500 m, then clamped — **three of the five tones**, with both boundaries stacked inside the 3 px a solution wall spends crossing them. Derived instead, the boundaries fall at 0.23, 0.64, 1.09 and 1.56 m. The rule is *flat, seam, flat*, so what is illegal is a **bare cut**: two plateaus meeting with no dithered pixel between them, on a field gentle enough to have afforded the seam. On a wall crossing a rung inside a pixel, a clean step between adjacent rungs is the only thing that can be drawn | `swampT` in `src/sunlight.js`; `dither` on the water rows of `src/surface.js`; asserted in `test/swamp.test.mjs`, 109 px bare without it and 12–22 px with it |
| **On a `dither:0` surface the crossing budget IS the design** | Follows from `rampStep` and the ramp length: a four-rung ramp's boundaries sit at `t = 1/6, 1/2, 5/6`, so a surface with dithering off changes colour only where a term carries it across one. A term smaller than its distance to a boundary draws nothing at all; a **low-frequency** term that crosses draws geology. Measured on the meadow sward: 4.2–9.6% of pixels sat off the modal grass rung, and holding each term flat in turn attributed almost all of it to the terrain normal rather than to the albedo — the largest single off-modal patch was **1069 px**, which is what read as "strange coloured areas" and as a hill nobody recognised as a hill. `relief 0.22` is the largest value at which no patch of 64 px or more survives at any of three seeds; the 4–5 px culm micro-relief is left crossing at 2–3%, because on this surface that crossing is the only texture the grass has | `BIOMES[].relief` and `BIOMES[].patch` in `src/biome.js`; `SURFACE[].ndl` and `.dither` in `src/surface.js`; asserted in `test/biome.test.mjs` |
| **A shadow is the size of its caster** | Undergrowth is too short for a projected shadow to describe, so it takes a contact ellipse instead. One 13 px blob served every short prop, which put a shadow two and a half times the plant's own width under a 5 px sedge tuft and made the shadow the plant. Rungs are 5, 9, 13, 17 and 21 px and a prop takes the widest rung at or below its width. Heights hold 5/13, the ratio the traveller's own 13x5 contact measures, so the ladder sits on one projection. **The rule is about height, not about being a prop.** It was written for undergrowth and applied only to props, so an actor under the floor fell through both branches and threw nothing at all: fennec 7 px, sandgrouse 6, viper 5, turtle 6, squirrel 6, boar 11, and the heron at 19 missing by a single row. Seven animals in four worlds sat on the ground without touching it. Any caster under the floor takes the ellipse, and anything over it (the traveller at 26, the camel at 43) casts its own silhouette and must not also take one. **Concession:** the 5 px rung projects to 1.92 rows, and a two-row ellipse loses its dither to the palette snap, so three rows is a declared legibility floor | `CONTACT_RUNGS`, `contactRung`, `contactRows` in `src/sprite.js`; `Scene.visible` picks the rung; asserted in `test/tracks.test.mjs`. Deferred for two sprints behind a frame-hash gate that no longer exists |
| **A shadow is thrown from the ground the caster stands over** | The projected pass measured height only WITHIN a sprite and rooted the cast on that sprite's own bottom row, which is right for anything standing on the ground and wrong for everything standing on something else. A meadow house's micro-turbine sits 86 rows up: it threw the shadow of a 27 px disc lying in the grass, 86 rows above the grass, computing a 17 px cast where 72 px is the answer. The windpump was worse in two directions at once, its ten 15 px bays falling under `uMinH` so a 10 m mast cast nothing, while each of them laid a contact ellipse at the row it was painted on and put ten shadows climbing the tower through open air. A caster now carries `iLift`, the drawn rows from the ground to its own foot: the root drops back to the ground, the shear runs `lift + height`, the legibility floor reads height above ground rather than sprite height, and a contact blob belongs only to something touching soil | `iLift` in `src/render.js` and `W_SHADOW_VS`; `lift` on the draw list in `src/scene.js`; `houseRotorLift` in `src/steading.js`; asserted in `test/steading.test.mjs` |
| **A building carries its own depth axis** | The world's depth axis draws straight up the screen, so its screen-x term is zero and a camera-facing wall has a side face zero pixels wide. Every drawn house was therefore a flat rectangle with a roof profile on it: `HOUSE_M.d = 4.5 m` is 68 px, which `obliqueRows` prices at 34 screen rows, and `makeHouse` drew none of them. A building instead mixes its depth up and to the RIGHT at 45°, halved on both screen axes, which is the cabinet variant of oblique projection. `obliqueRows` and `obliqueCols` are both `depthPx>>1`, so the receding edge steps 1:1 and the top face keeps exactly the rows it already had. **Concession:** the building's ground rectangle then draws as a parallelogram sheared `obliqueCols` right of world truth, so its footprint and collider follow the drawn base rather than the world one, and a building carrying a return may not be mirrored because the sun shears bottom-right and a mirrored return would light the side face brighter than the front | `obliqueCols` in `src/scale.js`; `cabinetBox` and the emitters in `src/construct.js`; `PROJECTION.md`; asserted in `test/construct.test.mjs` and `test/steading.test.mjs` |
| **A reflection is not a shadow** | A shadow records blocked illumination on a receiver; a water reflection replaces reflected sky with caster radiance mirrored at a contact anchor. The two must use separate targets and wet surfaces alone may sample the latter | `rtS` versus `rtR`, `W_REFLECT_VS`, and `W_WATER_FS`; asserted in `test/reflection.test.mjs` |

## Adding a row

State the law, cite it, and say where it is encoded. If the value came from
taste, say *taste*; if from a measurement off a reference image, say that. The
register is only useful if a reader can tell which rows they can trust and which
are somebody's opinion wearing a citation.

When a law moves from *available* to *encoded*, move the row and name the
implementation and test.
